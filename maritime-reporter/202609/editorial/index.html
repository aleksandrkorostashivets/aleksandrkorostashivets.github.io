Editorial

Photo Justin Zurre

In late July in Chicago at the American Great Lakes Port Association (AGLPA) annual meeting, I had more than an hour to sit with Stephen M. Carmel, Administrator, U.S. Maritime Administration (MarAd), for an interview that was broad and deep, with all roads leading back to the much-discussed U.S. maritime revitalization. I had been chasing Carmel’s public affairs teams for months for this interview, and was told that he prefers ‘in person’, which I genuinely respect as this is the recipe for the best stories and the most meaningful connections. Before we met in Chicago, I did not have any personal connection to Carmel, but when he got the job earlier this year the universal response from some of my most trusted colleagues in maritime was: “he’s the right man at the right time for the job.”

The litmus test on his effectiveness will only come with time, but he is clearly knowledgeable and passionate about the industry that he found by chance in his late teens. More importantly, he understands and advocates that the “maritime” revitalization is much more than a maritime issue: it’s a wholistic economic issue; it’s a pressing national security issue. It’s not simply about pumping money into new and existing shipyards, it’s about solving some very fundamental problems – particularly around cargo – and rebuilding the entirety of the maritime supply chain, for the benefit of both commercial, government and navy construction.

I said and written repeatedly that I’ve heard more about maritime from the Federal government in the last 18 months than the previous 32 years combined, and that is not exaggeration. The mandate is real, the policies are being put into place, the interest and dollars from overseas shipbuilders and maritime stakeholders are starting to flow in. Ultimately, Carmel sees the biggest challenge is overcoming entrenched attitudes:

"Our biggest weakness—our biggest challenge—is our own attitude. We can revitalize our shipbuilding industry. We can revitalize our maritime industry. But we're not going to get there if we abandon the fight before we take the field."

Gregory R. Trauthwein
