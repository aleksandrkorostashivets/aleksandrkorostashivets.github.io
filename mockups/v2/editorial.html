Editorial

Never before have I seen the rapidity of information flow from and around the U.S. maritime industry.

Today, mariners set a safety contour on their ECDIS based on their vessel’s draught, which delineates the safe navigable space. However, current ECDIS are limited to the predefined contour limits of S-57 ENCs, which are typically only available at broad intervals.

For bridge teams, more precise safety contours and an improved awareness of the true navigable space around their vessels can help to increase safety when navigating in shallow, confined or highly congested waterways.

Integration of S-102 with other interoperable S-100 data layers can provide further advantages. Combining S-102 with S-104, the data layer for water level information, can support dynamic adjustment of under keel clearance calculations in near real time.

Across both voyage planning and navigation, the interoperability of S-100 layers was cited as a key advantage. By integrating multiple data sources, crews could avoid cross-referencing materials such as tide tables and current references, improving efficiency.

“Simply put, the direction and success of commercial shipbuilding is not only preference cargo.”

Bob Kunkel
